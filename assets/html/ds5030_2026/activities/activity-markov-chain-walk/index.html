Markov Chain Walk

Type in a transition matrix or a joint table, then watch one random walk move through it

Each row of the table is today's state; each column is tomorrow's. You can enter a transition matrix, a joint table, or raw counts: the page divides every row by its row sum, so each row becomes the conditional PMF pr[tomorrow | today]. That row is all the chain uses to pick its next step.

The chain
Arrows go from today's state to tomorrow's, labeled with pr[tomorrow | today]; thicker arrows are more likely. The colored arrows leave the current state: they are the row the next step is drawn from. With more than three states, only those arrows are labeled.
Press Step to take the first step.
How a step is drawn: the current row splits [0, 1) into one piece per state, sized by its probability. A uniform random number u lands in one piece, and that piece is tomorrow.

Bars: the share of steps the walk has spent in each state so far. Black tick: the long-run share the transition matrix predicts from this start. Run +1,000 steps a few times and the bars settle onto the ticks.
Your table
States 2 up to 10
Rows: today. Columns: tomorrow. Entries can be fractions (1/3), decimals (0.25), or counts (86). Edit a state's name in the left column.
Transition matrix used
Each row divided by its row sum, so every row sums to 1: pr[tomorrow = column | today = row].
