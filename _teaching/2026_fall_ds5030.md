DS 5030 is a graduate course in the UVA School of Data Science that picks up the threads of students' math and probability education and builds a foundation for later work in machine learning and AI, grounded in data. Topics include linear algebra for data science, random variables and expectation, representing distributions (ECDFs, CDFs/PDFs, kernel density estimates), order statistics and extreme events, sampling distributions, the bootstrap and the CLT, joint and conditional distributions, conditional expectation, Markov chains, maximum likelihood models (failure times, counts, regression, and binary outcomes), and Bayesian inference.

The second half of the course builds standard modeling frameworks (Markov chains, maximum likelihood, Bayes), but every lab follows the same arc: explore the data non-parametrically first, then estimate the model, then do inference on its parameters, then predict. The bootstrap from Week 6 comes back in every assignment as the default way to measure how precisely a parameter or prediction is estimated.

Each week has a Tuesday math day (concept-driven lecture and quiz) and a Thursday lab day (a pre-lab bridge notebook, then a coding lab that starts in class). Students fork a [template repository](https://github.com/smgroves/UU_F26_templates) and pull new lecture notebooks, labs, and datasets each week; the datasets for every notebook below are in that repo.
